# From Islam to Christ — App Plan

## 1. Project Vision

Build a private, respectful, Bible-centered app for Muslims who are questioning Islam, exploring Christianity, or choosing to follow Jesus Christ.

The app should help a seeker move from curiosity to understanding, from fear to confidence, and from reading about Christianity to actually learning how to follow Jesus.

**Core idea:**  
> A safe path from questions to Christ.

The app should not feel like a debate archive or an attack platform. It should feel like a trusted guide: Scripture first, clear explanations, practical next steps, and strong privacy.

---

## 2. Primary Platforms

### MVP
- Android phone
- Responsive mobile-first interface

### Later
- iPhone / iOS
- Windows / macOS desktop app
- Web app
- Optional offline-only edition for high-risk users

---

## 3. Target Users

### A. Curious Muslim
Someone who has questions about Jesus, the Bible, the Trinity, salvation, or contradictions between Christianity and Islam.

### B. Secret Seeker
Someone privately researching Christianity who may face family, cultural, or social consequences if discovered.

### C. New Christian from a Muslim Background
Someone who already believes in Jesus but needs help understanding Christian doctrine, prayer, church, baptism, Scripture, and daily discipleship.

### D. Christian Mentor
A mature believer helping a Muslim-background seeker or new convert.

---

## 4. Core Product Principles

1. **Jesus-centered**
   - The central goal is to introduce the user to Jesus Christ through Scripture.

2. **Bible-first**
   - Bible passages should be accessible directly from lessons, articles, questions, and study tools.

3. **Private by design**
   - Privacy is not an optional feature; it is part of the core architecture.

4. **Respectful but clear**
   - Present Christian claims confidently without insulting Muslims as people.

5. **Simple before advanced**
   - A seeker should be able to begin without knowing Christian terminology.

6. **Offline-capable**
   - Important content should remain available without internet access.

7. **Progressive discipleship**
   - The app should guide users from basic questions toward Christian life and maturity.

## 33.1 MVP Product Boundary

### MVP working product

The first release is a **private Bible Explorer**. A user should be able to:

- open the app and understand its privacy limits;
- read one legally cleared Bible translation offline;
- browse books, chapters, and verses;
- search by Bible reference;
- open Scripture references from lessons and articles;
- complete a short Start Here journey introducing Jesus and the Gospel;
- read a small set of foundational articles;
- save bookmarks locally;
- resume local journey progress;
- adjust text size and appearance;
- protect private local state with a PIN or biometric lock; and
- delete local bookmarks, progress, and private data.

### Explicit MVP non-goals

The following remain outside the first release:

- AI question answering;
- mentor chat or pastoral conversations;
- church finder;
- public or moderated community features;
- cloud sync and account creation;
- audio and video libraries;
- translation comparison;
- interlinear tools and advanced word studies;
- testimony uploads;
- remote analytics; and
- social sharing of sensitive reading or journey activity.

These features may remain in the later roadmap, but they must not create backend, account, moderation, or telemetry requirements for the first release.

## 33.2 Canonical Information Architecture

The recommended MVP navigation is:

1. **Home** — continue reading, continue the journey, and Start Here.
2. **Bible** — translation selection, book/chapter navigation, reference search, and reader settings.
3. **Learn** — Questions, Christianity 101, Jesus, Bible Reliability, and Christianity-and-Islam comparison articles.
4. **Journey** — ordered lessons and progress.
5. **Saved** — bookmarks, highlights, and notes when those features are enabled.

Settings, privacy, and Discreet Mode should be reachable from a Profile or Settings entry, rather than requiring a sixth primary destination.

Questions are a content category within Learn for the MVP. Library is also a content category within Learn. This avoids making Home, Questions, Library, and Learn compete for the same content.

Mentor, AI, SOS, multiple-language, and audio controls should not appear as active MVP navigation destinations until the underlying feature is implemented and safety-reviewed. If they appear in a prototype, they must be visibly marked as unavailable or planned.

## 33.4 Privacy Threat Model and User Promise

Discreet Mode must be described as reducing casual discovery, not as guaranteeing complete secrecy. The app must explain that privacy can still be compromised by device access, the device PIN, operating-system records, backups, screenshots, external browsers, keyboards, or a compromised device.

The MVP privacy baseline is:

- no account required for reading or lessons;
- no remote analytics;
- no sensitive notification text by default;
- no private content written to shared or external storage by default;
- local bookmarks and progress only;
- local delete/reset controls;
- automatic locking after inactivity;
- PIN and optional biometric unlock;
- private content excluded from ordinary device backups, or protected by a documented encrypted-backup design;
- sensitive task previews and screenshots handled according to the documented privacy policy;
- a quick-close action that removes the app task where Android permits; and
- no claim that quick-close erases every operating-system trace.

The privacy design must document the following before release:

- application label and launcher-icon behavior;
- notification permissions and notification-history behavior;
- recent-apps task previews;
- screenshots and screen recording;
- clipboard and sharing behavior;
- crash reports and application logs;
- Android backup and restore behavior;
- encryption-at-rest and key-storage behavior;
- PIN failure and lockout behavior;
- biometric fallback behavior;
- data deletion behavior; and
- the limitations of calculator-style or neutral launch screens.

The user-facing privacy screen must describe these limits in plain language. A neutral identity is a safety aid, not a substitute for device security.

## 33.5 Content, Licensing, and Attribution Gate

No translation, commentary, lexicon, image, audio file, video, or source dataset may enter a release package until its redistribution status is recorded.

Create a content license manifest with at least these fields:

```text
asset_id
file_path
content_type
source
copyright_holder
license
redistribution_allowed
commercial_use_allowed
modification_allowed
required_attribution
required_notice
source_version
review_status
reviewed_by
reviewed_at
```

The raw `Data` directory is a research and conversion source, not an automatic application bundle. In particular:

- source DOCX files must not be assumed redistributable merely because they are present locally;
- scraped material requires provenance and redistribution review;
- Hebrew, Greek, Strong's, Text-Fabric, and lexicon resources must be reviewed by layer, because text, annotations, software, and dictionaries may carry different licenses;
- the application must expose required attribution and notices; and
- the build should fail when a packaged asset has no confirmed redistribution status.

The first release should use one cleared translation and defer advanced Hebrew/Greek resources until their runtime purpose and legal status are documented.

## 33.6 Content and Data Architecture

Bible content should be converted into a versioned, runtime-oriented format rather than read directly from DOCX, Text-Fabric, compressed MQL, or research files.

For the current React/Vite/Electron/Capacitor implementation, the repeatable conversion pipeline writes a versioned SQLite database at `public/data/from-darkness-to-light.db`. It is an internal runtime content layer, not a user-facing source-file browser. The raw `Data` directory remains a local conversion input; the renderer consumes normalized database rows and the desktop shell does not expose direct file browsing as primary navigation.

Each Bible passage should have stable identifiers and metadata:

```text
translation_id
book_id
book_order
chapter_number
verse_number
canonical_reference
text
heading
footnotes
source_version
license_status
```

Each article or journey lesson should have:

```text
id
title
slug
category
stage
level
summary
body
scripture_references
source_references
related_content
editorial_status
content_version
last_reviewed
```

User-generated state must be stored separately from immutable content. A bookmark should point to a stable content or passage identifier, not to a screen position or copied text string.

The conversion pipeline must be repeatable and must produce:

- normalized Bible data;
- searchable reference fields;
- validated book/chapter/verse relationships;
- resolved Scripture links;
- attribution metadata;
- a content version; and
- a report of rejected or ambiguous source records.

## 33.7 Recommended Technical Default

Because the MVP is Android-first and privacy/offline behavior is central, the recommended default is:

- Kotlin and Jetpack Compose;
- a local SQLite/Room database;
- local full-text search;
- platform-backed key storage for private user state;
- packaged content with explicit versioning;
- no backend dependency for reading, lessons, bookmarks, or progress; and
- no analytics in the MVP.

Flutter remains a valid alternative if iOS is a firm near-term requirement. That decision must be made before the application scaffold is created because it affects navigation, storage, authentication, accessibility, and platform privacy work.

The first technical decision record must name the chosen stack, minimum supported Android version, package identifier, build format, supported device range, and private-pilot distribution method.

## 33.8 MVP Acceptance Criteria

The MVP is not ready for a private pilot until all of the following are true:

- the app launches and displays core content with networking disabled;
- a user can navigate to a known book, chapter, and verse;
- reference search returns the correct passage;
- a Scripture reference in an article opens the correct passage;
- a bookmark persists after app restart and remains local;
- journey progress persists after app restart and remains local;
- no account is required for reading or lessons;
- no reading, search, question, bookmark, or journey event is sent to a server;
- private state is protected according to the approved threat model;
- notification, task-preview, screenshot, backup, and quick-close behavior has been tested on supported Android devices;
- local private data can be deleted;
- all packaged content has a completed license-manifest entry;
- attribution and required notices are accessible in the app;
- core content passes theological, source, cultural, safety, copy, and mobile-readability review;
- the app remains usable with large text and Android accessibility services enabled; and
- the privacy UI does not promise protection that the implementation cannot provide.

## 33.9 Implementation Order

Build in this order:

1. Complete the name, package, language, theological baseline, stack, privacy, and licensing decisions.
2. Create the Android application scaffold and basic navigation.
3. Build the content conversion pipeline for one translation and the initial article set.
4. Implement the Bible reader and structured Scripture references.
5. Implement Home, Learn, Journey, and local bookmarks/progress.
6. Implement local privacy controls, lock behavior, and private-state deletion.
7. Test offline operation, content integrity, accessibility, storage, and update behavior.
8. Run a small trusted private pilot.
9. Record pilot findings before adding highlights, notes, multiple translations, audio, AI, or community features.

## 33.10 Open Decisions Before Coding

The following decisions remain explicit gates rather than assumptions:

- final or temporary public name;
- permanent Android package identifier;
- first launch language and RTL/localization requirements;
- theological or confessional baseline;
- first legally cleared translation;
- Kotlin/Compose versus Flutter;
- exact definition of Discreet Mode;
- private-pilot distribution channel;
- content reviewers and approval authority; and
- whether the visual concept is a current MVP target or a later product vision.
